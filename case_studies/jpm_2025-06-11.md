# Case Study 02 — JPM, a quiet mid-June session (grade day 2025-06-11)

**Type:** no-catalyst day · **Ticker:** JPM · **asof:** 2025-06-11
**Notebook:** [`notebooks/case_jpm_2025-06-11.ipynb`](../notebooks/case_jpm_2025-06-11.ipynb)

---

## Why this case

The counterpoint to the two earnings days: **no material news**. JPMorgan doesn't
report until mid-July; the overnight coverage is a routine analyst reiteration and
a generic "banks await CPI" market wrap. There is nothing for the narrative signal
to read.

The question: **when narrative correctly abstains, does the day carry any
information — or is it just noise for both sides?**

## Inputs

- **Prices:** daily bars through the **2025-06-10** close. JPM was near all-time
  highs and had been drifting up.
- **Kronos:** Kronos-small, 200 paths, 120-bar lookback.
- **Headlines** (`case_studies/headlines/JPM_2025-06-11.json`): two routine items,
  deliberately non-material.

## Reading

**The headline sub-agent did its job.** It kept the analyst note, dropped the
generic market recap as immaterial, and the scorer judged the remaining item
"recycled sell-side commentary rather than a fresh catalyst" — neutral,
conviction 1. Narrative correctly stays out of the way.

**With narrative silent, the row is a Kronos calibration check.** A near-zero
realised move (−0.17%) landing close to the centre of a tight ±1.7% envelope is
the boring, correct outcome. Kronos's mild +0.33% lean was directionally "wrong"
but economically meaningless.

Over the month-long run, no-catalyst days are where you read envelope coverage
cleanly — no event to blow through the bands. Part 2 reports coverage split by
catalyst vs. no-catalyst.
