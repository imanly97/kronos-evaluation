# Case Study 03 — NFLX, Q1 2024 earnings (grade day 2024-04-19)

**Type:** narrative head-fake · **Ticker:** NFLX · **asof:** 2024-04-19
**Notebook:** [`notebooks/case_nflx_2024-04-19.ipynb`](../notebooks/case_nflx_2024-04-19.ipynb)

---

## Why this case

The overnight headlines were unambiguously strong. Netflix added **9.33M
subscribers** against a ~5M estimate, beat on EPS ($5.28 vs $4.52), grew revenue
15% YoY, and expanded margins. Any reasonable read of the news flow says *bull,
high conviction*.

**The stock closed down 9.1%.**

Two lines in the same release did the damage: Netflix said it would **stop
reporting quarterly subscriber numbers from 2025** — retiring the exact metric
the bull case had been built on — and guided Q2 revenue only in line to slightly
light. The market had been long the subscriber-growth story; the print removed
the scoreboard.

## Inputs

- **Prices:** daily bars through the **2024-04-18** close (Kronos context, no
  lookahead). NFLX had been grinding higher into the print.
- **Kronos:** Kronos-small, 200 paths, 120-bar lookback, `pred_len=3`, graded on
  bar 1.
- **Headlines** (`case_studies/headlines/NFLX_2024-04-19.json`, curated + cited):
  the subscriber beat, the EPS beat, the end of subscriber disclosure, and the
  in-line-to-light Q2 revenue guide.

## Reading

**Narrative was confidently wrong, and it saw the reason.** The scorer's own
rationale reads: *"offset somewhat by … the surprise decision to stop disclosing
quarterly subscriber counts … and Q2 revenue guidance … in-line to slightly
light … Net effect strongly positive given the magnitude of the beat."* It
identified the disclosure change as an offset and still weighted the beat
higher. That is exactly a head-fake: the news genuinely was good, the market had
already priced it, and the fine print mattered more.

**Structure was also wrong, and its envelope was too narrow.** Kronos, blind to
the release and reading an uptrend, projected +1.13% with 80% odds of an up close.
The realised −9.09% sat well below its 5th percentile — a coverage failure driven
entirely by information the model had no way to see.

**No divergence row.** Both signals pointed up, so there is no
divergence-outcome to adjudicate — but the day is a clean −1 for both the
direction-hit rate and envelope coverage. The pilot lesson: discount narrative
conviction when the news is loud and one-directional, and don't expect a
price-only envelope to contain an event-driven gap.
