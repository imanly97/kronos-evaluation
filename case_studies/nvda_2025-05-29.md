# Case Study 01 — NVDA, Q1 FY2026 earnings (grade day: 2025-05-29)

**Type:** scheduled-catalyst · **Ticker:** NVDA · **asof:** 2025-05-29
**Status:** planned template — real inputs fixed below; model outputs marked `[TO GENERATE]`.

---

## Why this case

It isolates a clean asymmetry. Kronos is fed prices **through the May 28 close**, which is
*before* the earnings release (NVDA reported after the bell). So the structural model is
constitutionally blind to the single biggest driver of the next day's move. The narrative
agent, reading the overnight earnings coverage, has exactly that catalyst. If narrative
anticipates a move that sits in the tail of Kronos's price-only distribution, this is the
canonical "narrative sees what structure can't" illustration — and it teaches a real lesson
about foundation-model limits around scheduled events.

It is also, deliberately, narrative's *easiest* case. That's why the plan pairs it with a
no-catalyst day and a head-fake day (see PLAN §14).

## Divergence hypothesis

Kronos, blind to the after-hours release, should center its 05-29 distribution near a normal
daily move with a wide-but-symmetric envelope. The **actual** reaction (a sharp gap-up) is
likely to land at or beyond the upper 95th percentile of that price-only envelope. Narrative,
having read the beat, should call direction correctly with high conviction.

**Predicted verdict:** *narrative-wins, directional + magnitude divergence* — structure
assigns the realized up-move low probability; narrative anticipates it. **To be confirmed by
the run, not assumed.** If Kronos's envelope happens to contain the move, that's an equally
interesting (and honest) result about how much dispersion the model already prices in.

### What actually happened vs the hypothesis

The predicted verdict was *narrative-wins, directional + magnitude divergence*.
The run says **aligned**. Two reasons, both instructive:

1. **The close-to-close move was only +3.25%**, not +6%. The gap-up faded through
   the session. It still sat near the top of Kronos's envelope (~90th percentile),
   inside but not central.
2. **Kronos was not neutral.** NVDA went into the print on a sharp recovery off
   the April lows, and the model extrapolated that momentum: median +1.79%,
   P(up) 85%. Structure "guessed right" for a reason unrelated to the earnings —
   and the narrative scorer independently landed bull/7 on the beat.

This is the honest-null branch the plan anticipated: *"If Kronos's envelope
happens to contain the move, that's an equally interesting result about how much
dispersion the model already prices in."* It also shows why the exemplar needs
the no-catalyst ([`jpm_2025-06-11`](jpm_2025-06-11.md)) and head-fake
([`nflx_2024-04-19`](nflx_2024-04-19.md)) partners — one clean beat where both
signals happened to agree tells us almost nothing on its own.

## Reader takeaway (draft)

Foundation models that read only price cannot know an earnings release is coming after the
bell; on scheduled-catalyst days, a price-blind narrative signal has a structural edge. The
honest corollary — and the reason this can't be the whole story — is that this is narrative's
friendliest possible setup. The interesting question is what happens on days with *no* catalyst,
and on days when the headlines are loud but wrong. Those are the next two case studies.
